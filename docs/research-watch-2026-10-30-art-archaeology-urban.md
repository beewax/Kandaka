### Priority 1 — Sudan-specific official evidence, institutional method, and fieldwork

**SDN-2026-WASH-44 — UNICEF Sudan, Humanitarian Mid-Year Situation Report No.44, Jan–June 2026; publication August 2026.**
- Publisher: https://www.unicef.org/sudan/reports/unicef-sudan-humanitarian-mid-year-situation-report-2026
- **Original primary operational report, PDF sections read** (publisher text; PDF page render attempted separately). Reports 120 urban water facilities newly built or rehabilitated, including solarization, with **2.2m people reached**, and a Nyala subset of about **600,000** (not 2.8m combined). Water-sector capacity findings include functioning institutions estimated down from ~80% before the war to 45–55% and fewer than 3% of states reporting adequate O&M allocations. These are **UNICEF/sector monitoring estimates**, not independent audits of sustained water delivery. The WASH core pipeline budget must NOT be misreported as the cost of the 120 facilities.
- **Affected** A06 urban transport/planning; energy resilience; A01 development planning; A09 aid-delivery measures.
- **Exact recommended change:** every proposed 5–10-minute service cell should define hours of available potable water, accessibility/walking distance, drainage, electricity autonomy, technicians, spare parts, tariff/municipal maintenance budgets and arrangements for displaced residents. Treat solarized local water infrastructure as a measured recovery option, not evidence that service is continuously reliable.
- **License:** public UNICEF report, no verified CC reuse permission; **link only; no PDF or image mirroring**.

**SDN-2026-ARCH-PEDAGOGY — Ahmed Hussein Abdelrahman Adam, “Pedagogy Under Fire: Teaching Archaeology with WhatsApp during Sudan’s Civil War,” `Public Humanities`, 18 June 2026, DOI 10.1017/pub.2026.10190.**
- https://doi.org/10.1017/pub.2026.10190
- **Full publisher HTML read, authored first-person case account, CC BY 4.0.** University of Khartoum's lecturers used compressed PDF handouts, asynchronous audio lessons, group discussion, grade records and remote assessment when LMS/servers and travel failed. Author also reports this model at University of Bahri. Actual student participation is described qualitatively, **no comparative achievement effect or per-student delivery cost demonstrated**. Field excavation/practical training was explicitly not replaceable by mobile teaching.
- **Affected:** A17 historical/digital-heritage discussion *in future revisions only*; potential stand-alone archaeology-education/back-up methods article; University reconstruction.
- **Exact recommended change:** create a small, Sudanese-led archaeology training continuity protocol: consent-based cohorts, low-bandwidth audio+text, downloadable legal readings, off-device encrypted records/backups, priority assistance for students without devices, no field deployment into unsafe areas and later make-up practical training. Measure actual access, completion, gender/displacement inclusion, learning and cost. This is a **quick win**. Software free, phones/data/power/moderation and accessibility NOT free. Cheaper fallback: offline flash cards/printed handouts and audio voice calls where data unavailable.

**SDN-2026-COMMUNITY-ARCH — Mohamed Bashir, “Pluriversal Pasts: Decolonising Archaeological Practice in Sudan through the Integration of Folklore and Community Epistemologies,” `Cambridge Archaeological Journal`, 29 September 2026, DOI 10.1017/S0959774326100742.**
- https://doi.org/10.1017/S0959774326100742
- **Publisher abstract and metadata verified; full methods/results NOT reviewed in this pass. Publisher indicates CC BY 4.0.** Based on fieldwork around the Third/Fifth Cataracts; community memory, place names, water names and custodianship help guide investigation and safeguard sites, but are not automatic proof of dates or historical continuity.
- **Affected:** Nubia/Kush/Funj site-management backlog; future addendum to already approved A17; archaeological methodology/library candidate.
- **Exact recommended change:** require optional, compensated community-knowledge interviews and multilingual toponym inventories alongside mapped archaeological evidence; record consent/knowledge restrictions, do not publicly reveal sensitive site GPS without review, assign joint custodianship and validate chronological hypotheses independently. Quick win for field protocols, five-year investment for NCAM training and archives. Cost unspecified; expensive alternative is full-site survey without local co-production; cheaper baseline is existing community interviews and a structured paper record. Risks extractive research, confidentiality, site looting, interpretive overclaim.
- **License:** CC BY 4.0 per publisher metadata; link until full article and image rights reviewed.

**SDN-2026-UNESCO-HEF — UNESCO Heritage Emergency Fund, “Urgent Safeguarding Response for the Damaged Collections at the Sudan National Museum,” 2026.**
- https://www.unesco.org/en/culture-emergencies/heritage-emergency-fund/urgent-safeguarding-response-damaged-collections-sudan-national-museum
- **Original project description, not completion report; budget USD 50,000**. Museum annex store has over 10,000 objects (an exposure count, NOT objects conserved); UNESCO proposes emergency assessment, inventory, prioritized conservation, roof/wall repair, safe shelving. Do not present project as completed or calculate cost/object.
- **Affected:** A17 (already individually approved; **do not edit without preserving approval gate**); culture/archaeological conservation workplan; museum small-grant cases.
- **Exact recommendation:** use $50,000 as an identified targeted *intervention envelope* rather than price for reconstructing the museum. Before a replication estimate, request output and procurement reports, conservation scope, site safety clearances and a preservation/maintenance plan. Quick win only when safe access and expert conservators are available; longer-term national inventory and dispersed backups. Cheaper alternative in unsafe settings is remote condition/custody documentation and emergency stabilization where feasible.
- **License:** UNESCO website no confirmed CC image/document reproduction grant; link only.

### Priority 2 — Implemented comparative cases, with Sudan adaptation scorecards

**INT-URB-KIBERA — Kibera Public Space Project (Nairobi, Kenya).**
- Case research published **31 Jan 2025**, KDI + African Cities Research Consortium: https://african-cities-database.org/urc-record-index/KPSP/
- **Implemented:** 12 community-managed public spaces of ~100–1000 m² by late 2024, integrating flood/drainage elements, water/sanitation, livelihood kiosks, youth/women's civic spaces. **Construction-only aggregate $630,000** for sites across years; excludes salaries, design and long-run O&M. No independent causal impacts inferred.
- **Sudan:** begin with one flood/heat-vulnerable block within 5–10-minute walk of school, clinic, water point and market. Co-design shaded accessible footpaths, safe drainage, public toilets if maintainable and a vendor rental/municipal O&M compact. Explicit anti-eviction/tenure and flood-safety safeguards; retain dust-appropriate locally available surfacing. Municipal public-works engineer, drainage expert, local masons, waste management and civil-society co-management required.
- **Failure risks:** flood exposure, unsafe land tenure, exclusion, failed sanitation upkeep, volunteer burden, capture of vendor space, insecurity. **Cheaper alternative:** de-silt an existing ditch, clear litter, add accessible short path and shade before building an integrated plaza. **Tier:** quick-win micro-pilot, 5-year wider network. **License:** no verified image/text redistribution grant, link only.

**INT-URB-SOMALIA — Somalia Urban Resilience Project II, World Bank Results (10 June 2026).**
- https://www.worldbank.org/en/results/2026/06/10/building-resilient-cities-and-creating-jobs-in-somalia
- **Implemented through 2025, project-report indicators:** 39 km roads and 45 km sidewalks across seven cities, nearly 700,000 with improved access, >526,000 worker-days for 15,000 workers, **12% construction workers women**, 16% IDPs. Measures reported by implementer, not independent counterfactual. World Bank source does NOT give verified per-km or full treatment cost; no invented unit prices.
- **Sudan:** local councils with procurement/financial safeguards and safe-access engineering; focus on missing **walking links, crossings and drainage to everyday services**, not just resurfaced motor roads. Hire and train women, displaced people and local apprentices; publish job distribution and O&M budgets. Municipal finance, GIS, road safety/disabled-access design, community consultation and contracting needed.
- **Risks:** procurement capture, displacement, road-first spending, upkeep deficits, security and flood disruption. **Cheaper alternative:** repair 50–200m sidewalk gaps, crossings and drainage in an existing service corridor rather than paving full streets. **Tier:** five-year municipal program following quick pilots. **Rights:** World Bank results page link only; license for embedded photos not independently checked.

**INT-URB-SANTIAGO — Paseo Bandera street conversion, Santiago (Chile), 2026 peer-reviewed post-implementation thermal study.**
- Cárdenas-Jirón & Elgueta-Strange (18 May 2026), `Revista AUS`, DOI 10.4206/aus.2026.n39-02: https://doi.org/10.4206/aus.2026.n39-02
- **Original eight-page PDF text reviewed; figure rendering not confirmed.** Site implemented 2017–18 with pedestrian-only surface, artwork and seats; authors measured 3 heatwave days (Jan 2024), found **physiological equivalent temperature >41°C** mid-afternoon, and ~15°C higher PET relative to open reference at a point. **PET is a thermal comfort metric, NOT observed 41°C ambient air temperature.** This is localized small-sample evidence, not general climatic effect everywhere. Original sponsorship makes all-in USD cost unknown, not literally free.
- **Sudan:** for 5–10-minute walkable centers mandate **measured pedestrian-level radiant/air thermal comfort, shade continuity, wind, dust, water and maintenance**, plus disability access. A colorful pedestrian plaza without shade may become unusable. Use durable cheap shade (awnings/arcades), appropriate native planting only where water/roots safe, select materials not just bright paint. City street permits, artists, engineers, community groups, small climate-monitoring kit required; avoid surveillance technology not essential to the pilot. **Cheaper alternative:** temporary removable market-stall shade and a few accessible shaded crossings, not comprehensive art/streetworks. **Tier: quick win test, 5-year network**.
- **License warning:** publisher's homepage lists **CC BY-NC-ND** while submissions policy mentions **CC BY**; article-specific license unverified/conflicting. Link only and do not rehost PDF/photos until resolved.

**INT-URB-BOGOTA — Barrios Vitales: San Felipe, Bogotá, Colombia.**
- Municipal confirmation (updated 26 September 2025): https://www.movilidadbogota.gov.co/barrios-vitales
- WRI practice note, **5 Sep 2025** (63 pages), DOI 10.46830/wripn.23.00085: https://es.wri.org/publicaciones/aire-limpio-en-barrios-vitales
- **Implemented neighborhood street-reallocation pilot in Jan–Feb 2022; practice outcome/measurement report reviewed through WRI publisher abstract and conclusions**. Better pedestrian zones, delivery bays and local-commerce access. WRI reported **40% higher modeled vehicle emissions in Feb 2023 versus Feb 2022** amid post-pandemic traffic rebound, plus lower trends for some pollutants confounded by climate/economics; **not reliable proof the street conversion reduced emissions**. Do not cite secondary unverified '46% crash reduction' as an independently audited outcome. Cost undisclosed.
- **Sudan:** pick one service-cell, measure footfall, 5–10 min safe essential-service access, modal/travel time, collisions, air quality/dust and shade baseline/followup. Road authority, municipal traffic engineers, market businesses, accessibility advocates required; accessible crossings, transparent loading zones, vendor protections and no forced evictions. **Cheaper alternative:** intersections and short paved sidewalks with local shade/traffic calming rather than wholesale car ban. **Tier: quick-win reversible pilot, five-year implementation**. **WRI page says 'Creative Commons' without exact version; confirm individual PDF scope before copying figures.** Link only until verified.

**INT-HEAT-AHMEDABAD — Ahmedabad cool roofs, India (NRDC 22 May 2023).**
- https://www.nrdc.org/stories/climate-strategy-india-scales
- **Implemented:** pilot treatments reported for 3,000 roofs; city outlay **$10,450** and separately indicative material/coating costs **$0.50–$2 per square foot**. These are *different cost scopes* and CANNOT be reconciled into an all-in household construction rate. Observed cooling sometimes ~9°F in examples, not guaranteed for every Sudan roof.
- **Sudan:** pilot lime-whitewash or appropriate local reflective roof coatings on school/clinic/low-income roofs with test/reference indoor readings; ensure roof structure, waterproofing and rain shedding; inspect dusty soiling and annual coating renewal. Municipal health/education + local painters, roof specialist and community leaders needed. **Risks:** leakage, dust reflectance decay, unsafe roofs, absent maintenance. **Cheaper alternative:** locally applied limewash/ventilated shade structures before proprietary coatings. **Tier: quick win**. **License:** NRDC copyrighted content; link only.

**INT-RURAL-SOMALIA — Barwaaqo, Somalia Water for Rural Resilience (World Bank project account 8 May 2026).**
- https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2026/05/08/turning-water-access-into-jobs-and-livelihoods-lessons-from-barwaaqo-in-somalia
- **Implemented through project midpoint**: 40 new/rehabilitated multi-use water points, ~70,000 Farmer Field School participants, **>630,000 animals treated**; women 31% trained community animal-health workers. Does **not** document net producer income impact or unit cost, and these are implementation indicators rather than independent causal evaluation.
- **Sudan:** livestock/pastoral agriculture article: combined water point, grazing governance, veterinary access, women community animal-health apprentices and repairable solar pumping only if groundwater/quality supports it. Local pastoral associations, water-engineers, para-vets, ministry animal health and water authority required; track dry-season yield, pump downtime, herd survival, route conflict and access by nomadic groups. **Risks:** groundwater depletion, grazing conflict, veterinary supply shortages, maintenance underfunding. **Cheaper alternative:** rehabilitate existing hafirs and deploy periodic mobile vet visits instead of a new borehole. **Tier: five-year investment, quick-win rehab where justified.** **Rights:** World Bank page link only, no photo license checked.
