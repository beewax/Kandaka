### A13 — businesses between farms and households

- Starting Library source: [IFPRI THABAT enterprise baseline, Working Paper 29](https://hdl.handle.net/10568/184053), July 2026. Read the full report's executive summary, methods and limitations through its [publisher PDF](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/d286e5ce-32c4-4e2e-a0a9-46beae4c3655/content). It records 100 completed interviews from 180 shortlisted enterprises, nonrandom selection, self-reporting limitations and grant exposure before some baseline interviews. Thus no clean causal programme-effect claim, national representativeness claim or extrapolated enterprise count.
- Cross-check: [UNDP/ITC SAMRA](https://www.undp.org/sudan/publications/restoring-trade-and-livelihoods-sudans-agri-food-and-market-resilience-analysis-samra), including [report methods and section 2.3](https://www.undp.org/sites/g/files/zskgke326/files/2026-07/undp_sudan_samra.pdf). Selection of seven chains does not imply wheat, cotton or sugar are unimportant. Access restrictions and data gaps matter. Reports sharing background evidence are not independent confirmations of programme effectiveness.
- Fresh dated institutional check: [FAO GIEWS Sudan brief](https://cb.apps.fao.org/country.jsp?code=SDN&lang=en), reference date 24 September 2026. Supports dated disruption/input-cost context, not project costs or commercial viability. Refresh at release.
- Kandaka's contributions: explain working capital, distinguish grants from loans, repair services rather than count buildings, test inclusion/competition, measure consumer and worker gains. These are proposals, not claimed experimental findings. Opening example is explicitly hypothetical, not invented reporting.
- Existing livestock and infrastructure articles overlap in systems thinking but do not provide this enterprise-centred recovery and evaluation explainer. Do not turn this into a duplicate livestock industrialisation plan.

### A14 — clothing and authority

- Starting Library source: [Costumes of prestige and authority in Christian Nubia](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/costumes-of-prestige-and-authority-in-christian-nubia-insights-from-archaeological-reconstruction/E18904093E1C654A2F66572470D0C283), Antiquity, March 2026. Full HTML project report read. Five reconstructions: two kings, two royal mothers and a bishop. Successful reconstruction establishes feasibility of an interpretation, not exact identity with a historical garment or a medieval audience's feelings.
- Museum context: National Museum in Warsaw [Faras Gallery](https://www.mnw.art.pl/en/collections/permanent-galleries/faras-gallery/) and [collection description](https://www.mnw.art.pl/o-muzeum/zbiory-studyjne/zbiory-sztuki-wschodniochrzescijanskiej/galeria-faras/). Paintings, rescue context and local adaptation; historical descriptions are not current inventories of objects in wartime Sudan.
- Different method: [2021 textile dye analysis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jasrep.2021.103098), publisher preview and [Copenhagen research abstract](https://researchprofiles.ku.dk/da/publications/textile-dyeing-in-medieval-sudan-evidenced-by-hplc-ms-analyses-ma/). 59 samples; chemical analysis informs dyeing/material possibilities. Not a full technical replication. Shared author Magdalena Wozniak and the newer project's citation of this study are disclosed: institutional mirrors do not multiply independent evidence.
- Do not infer all women's equality from royal mothers, equate medieval royal mothers with earlier Kushite kandakes, infer political subordination from visual borrowing, or claim uninterrupted continuity with modern clothing. Alternative explanations for changes in artistic style remain open.
- Distinct from the existing Karanog/Dongola monuments and ancient queens articles: material culture, reconstruction and reading representations of authority are the focus.

### A15 — farmer-centred rebuilding

- Starting Library source: [IFPRI Farming in Crisis, Working Paper 27](https://hdl.handle.net/10568/182880) and [publisher catalogue](https://sudan.ifpri.info/publications/). Initial PDF access failed, but on 11 October the publisher catalogue supplied a [working full report](https://cgspace.cgiar.org/server/api/core/bitstreams/c1849f3b-34ed-4d9f-92c9-2548f7e642e9/content). Methods pp. 2–4, conclusions and limitations pp. 48–50 checked, including rendered PDF inspection. The telephone survey excludes Darfur; women were poorly represented; interview totals differ between methods prose and table. Both editions now disclose these limits without reproducing an unreliable precise sample total. No microdata reanalysis or causal claim was added. The earlier unavailable-PDF note is superseded.
- Library triangulation: [FAO remote-sensing assessment](https://www.fao.org/platforms/water-scarcity/Knowledge/knowledge-products/detail/a-remote-sensing-assessment-of-cultivated-cropland-area-in-the-sudan-during-the-summer-season/en), summer cultivated area 2018–2024. Area is not yield, net household income, secure tenure or food access.
- SAMRA and the dated September 2026 FAO brief above provide separate market and recent seasonal context, not proof of any proposed support package. Survey year and publication year remain distinct.
- Kandaka's proposals: safe voluntary access, locally timed support, grants versus seasonal credit, inclusion of tenants/workers/displaced people, net income rather than headline output, and evaluation that accounts for weather and starting conditions. No improvised land adjudication or unsafe explosive-clearance advice.
- Distinct from the existing agricultural-paradox essay and A13: household decisions, crop calendars and the capacity to farm again, not an export strategy or enterprise-centred programme.
