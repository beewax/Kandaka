# Academic research and fact-check report

**Article:** “Karanog and Dongola’s Throne Hall: Two Monuments, One Nubian History”  
**Research cut-off:** 19 September 2026  
**Scope:** supplied English draft, all nine image references, linked and newly retrieved scholarship. No repository changes or publication were made.

## Editorial assessment

The article’s central argument about Nubian participation in cultural exchange can be retained. Its strongest factual problems are the cattle/herder reading of plate 43, the blanket description of plate 107 as incised, and the minimum seven-century interval. More consequential interpretive revisions concern the G187 statue’s identification and restored appearance, the range of Karanog’s chronology, and the repeated treatment of Dongola’s original royal function as settled after briefly acknowledging uncertainty.

The grave totals are substantially faithful to the 1910 report. The conversion date of 1317 is strongly supported by the foundation inscription, and the published end date of mosque use is 1969. Those supported figures should remain, with their evidential status clear.

The claim table contains **108 records**: 24 supported, 71 supported with qualification, 5 contradicted, 2 uncertain, 6 unverified. These counts are editorial units, not independent statistical observations: repeated claims and grouped interpretations are disclosed. “Supported with qualification” is not a claim that every argument is proven.

## Method and source hierarchy

The original English draft is preserved separately. Exact claim excerpts were checked against it after HTML-entity decoding. Quantitative assertions, captions, rights statements, bibliography links and substantive interpretive premises were separated where their evidence differs. Rhetorical framing is labelled as interpretation rather than assigned spurious factual certainty. Headings, descriptive transitions, aesthetic judgments and front-matter publishing metadata are not treated as new historical evidence; their factual premises are audited in the table.

Primary excavation observations were checked against the 1910 text and plates, and settlement dates against Woolley’s 1911 volume. These sources preserve unique evidence but also contain racial hierarchies, Blemmyan identifications and evolutionary judgments which this audit does not adopt. Later typological, epigraphic and art-historical arguments were evaluated as interpretations with their own limits. Current museum and project records were checked separately from historic distribution lists. No dates were silently harmonized across conflicting sources.

For Dongola, the guide, detailed 2013 conservation report, 2012 field report, inscription database, and 2025 community scholarship were compared. **A ninth-century construction date, a Christian decorative history, a proposed royal function and an inscribed mosque foundation are different propositions.** None should be made to stand in for all the others.

## Most important evidence distinctions

1. **Original and modern chronology:** the 1910 conclusion on p. 84 is narrower at the beginning than the broad summary on p. 83. Williams’s regional chronology does not independently re-date every Karanog burial. Török’s early first-century BCE cemetery development on p. 69 prevents second–third century CE from serving as a complete site history. Penn’s E7089 catalogue independently retains a broad 300 BCE–100 CE date; catalogue metadata is not a resolution of the debate. [S01, pp. 81, 83–84](https://archive.org/download/karangromanonu00wool/karangromanonu00wool.pdf); [S04, pp. 17–20](https://oi-idb-static.uchicago.edu/multimedia/712/oine8pt1.pdf); [S05, p. 69](https://www.sag-online.de/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/Toeroek2002_KinshipAndDecorum-MeroiticElite_60-84_MittSAG13_small.pdf); [S07](https://collections.penn.museum/collections/object/156768)

2. **Statue, association and restoration:** the figure in plate 1 is not a securely identified king found intact in G187. Recovery in disturbed ground and assignment to G187 must be stated separately. Later identification as Maloton/Meleton, a peseto, does not make every restored attribute reliable. Quertinmont’s 2017 Aswan location is a dated scholarly statement, not a verified 2026 museum inventory. [S01, pp. 37–38, 47](https://archive.org/download/karangromanonu00wool/karangromanonu00wool.pdf); [S09, p. 63, figs. 2–3](https://www.penn.museum/documents/publications/expedition/35-2/Behind.pdf); [S10, fig. 1, note 7](https://koregos.org/en/arnaud-quertinmont-la-statuaire-funeraire-des-dignitaires-provinciaux-de-lempire-meroitique/)

3. **Architecture and use:** Godlewski’s royal interpretation explains aspects of the plan, but a church remains an alternative in 2025 scholarship. Four columns, wall pilasters, beam fragments and the eastern-corridor apse are evidence; a raised roof, terrace and specific audience ceremony include reconstruction. [S13, pp. 43–47](https://pcma.uw.edu.pl/wp-content/uploads/template/main/file/Dongola_guide/Chapters/9.Dongola_Throne_hall.pdf); [S15, pp. 260–261](https://bazhum.muzhp.pl/media/texts/polish-archaeology-in-the-mediterranean/2013-tom-22/polish_archaeology_in_the_mediterranean-r2013-t22-s248-272.pdf); [S19, p. 106](https://doi.org/10.37343/uw.2083-537X.pam34.2.6)

4. **Painting phases and identification:** later warrior saints and other fragments must not be packaged as one original ninth-century programme. The white enthroned figures are probably elders; 24 is the expected textual number, not a surviving count. The crowned person is unidentified. [S15, pp. 259–260](https://bazhum.muzhp.pl/media/texts/polish-archaeology-in-the-mediterranean/2013-tom-22/polish_archaeology_in_the_mediterranean-r2013-t22-s248-272.pdf); [S16, pp. 191–192](https://bazhum.muzhp.pl/media/texts/swiatowit-rocznik-poswiecony-archeologii-przeddziejowej-i-badaniom-pierwotnej-kultury-polskiej-i-sowianskiej/2012-tom-10-51-numer-a/swiatowit_rocznik_poswiecony_archeologii_przeddziejowej_i_badaniom_pierwotnej_kultury_polskiej_i_slowianskiej-r2012-t10_51-na-s185-192.pdf)

5. **Conversion and duration:** DBMNT 611 records 16 Rabīʿ I 717 AH / 29 May 1317. The stela does not independently date every rebuilt opening or plaster layer. The endpoints 1317 and 1969 yield 652 elapsed years; they do not prove uninterrupted worship year by year. A royal-use comparison additionally assumes the disputed function. [S17](https://dbmnt.uw.edu.pl/texts/611/); [S14, p. 137](https://www.pcma.uw.edu.pl/wp-content/uploads/template/main/file/Dongola_guide/Chapters/18.Dongola_Kingdom_of_Dongola_Town.pdf); [S15, p. 252, table 1; pp. 261–263](https://bazhum.muzhp.pl/media/texts/polish-archaeology-in-the-mediterranean/2013-tom-22/polish_archaeology_in_the_mediterranean-r2013-t22-s248-272.pdf); [S19, p. 106](https://doi.org/10.37343/uw.2083-537X.pam34.2.6)

6. **Historical and digital images:** the 1910 plates were checked directly, but no supplied PNG/JPG was available. The Commons historic view’s current revision is a documented 2020 DeepAI colourisation; its colours are not Cailliaud’s evidence. A modern cutaway cannot inherit public-domain status from an ancient building or an old engraving. [S02](https://mc.dlib.nyu.edu/files/books/ifa_egypt000402/ifa_egypt000402_lo.pdf); [S22](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Dongola_Throne_Hall.jpg); [S23](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Throne_Hall_of_Dongola,_1821.jpg); [S24](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/); [S25](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ15a.pdf)

## What the revision changes

The revised article retains the title, author, date, category, tags, draft status and main narrative sections. It keeps the argument about agency while qualifying claims about ethnicity, chronology, royal function, painting phases and preservation. It adds identifiable source links close to the relevant passages. It identifies Penn’s offering table and adds Sudanese-authored community scholarship.

All nine original image positions and paths are preserved as inactive comments, with corrected draft captions. The cover image field is empty rather than pointing at an unverified asset. No commissioning, ownership, “no changes” or blanket derivative-rights assertion is published as fact. The comments are an editorial device, not a requirement to abandon these images: the audit specifies what is needed to activate them.
