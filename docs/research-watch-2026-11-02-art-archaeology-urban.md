## Priority 1: original Sudan archaeological dataset (new to Kandaka)

**SUM — Osman Khaleel, Sol Sánchez-Dehesa Galán & Nuno Bicho, “The Sudan Middle Stone Age Lithic and Contextual Database (SUM): Structure, Research Potential, and Future Directions,” Journal of Open Archaeology Data, published 14 October 2026.**
- Original publication/DOI: https://doi.org/10.5334/joad.215 ; publisher-hosted complete HTML: https://reference-global.com/article/10.5334/joad.215?tab=article ; original dataset repository: https://osf.io/z3t62/overview
- Verification: complete publisher-hosted HTML and dataset documentation read. **The OSF underlying CSV/Excel was not opened because the direct site returned 403**, so no independent reanalysis or exact-row integrity audit. Publication and dataset **CC BY 4.0** are explicit; individual third-party images/reproductions still require rights review.
- Substance: consistent geospatial, chronological, technological, environmental and source metadata for **283 Sudanese Middle Stone Age sites**; **only 17 have absolute dates**. Accompanying raw/cleaned sheets, codebook, provenance and reliability fields. Includes Sudanese institution/NCAM participation. It is an archaeological research dataset, not proof that 283 sites are secure, excavated or chronologically fixed.
- Affected Kandaka: Archaeology/Nubia/Kush/Funj research backlog and future heritage-library methodological bibliography; A17 *Protecting Sudan’s Memory* is individually approval-gated and must not be edited as part of this watch.
- **Exact update recommendation:** add a source-linked English/Arabic Library record with date, DOI and CC BY; draft separate evidence-note protocol for research reuse. Cite quantified dating deficit (17/283), differentiate site discovery, absolute dating, surface finds and excavation reliability; use community-led quality checks with NCAM. Avoid exposing exact site coordinates as public maps in conflict/looting conditions, even though the dataset is openly licensed. Basic reading and source registry **quick win**; safe research infrastructure/curation **five-year**. Skills: Sudanese archaeologists, NCAM, GIS and curators, site security. Costs: dataset access is open; safe stewardship staff/time and hosting unpriced. Cheaper alternative: DOI link, citation and restricted review workflow instead of mirroring the coordinate tables. Risk: misdated sites, looting, decontextualization.

## Priority 2: Sudan-specific urban hazards and live methodology (previously published, newly discovered)

**Mohammed, Eltijani, Szabó and Szűcs, “Large-scale flood susceptibility mapping along major tributaries of the Nile River system in Sudan using convolutional neural network,” Scientific Reports, first online 20 June 2026, DOI 10.1038/s41598-026-58194-7.**
- Original: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-58194-7 ; publisher: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-58194-7 ; institution metadata https://pure.kfupm.edu.sa/en/publications/large-scale-flood-susceptibility-mapping-along-major-tributaries-/
- Verification: publisher abstract, metadata and methods summary reviewed; **full article and model robustness not independently reanalysed**. Publisher indicates **CC BY 4.0** with third-party exclusions. Input HDX flood polygons and 12 terrain/hydrology/land-use factors; fivefold cross-validation and 97% reported classification accuracy. Predicted higher susceptibility in Khartoum river confluence, river corridors and Gezira/Sennar alluvial plains. A predictive susceptibility model is **not** a reliable probability that a given address will flood; accuracy must be checked against spatial holdout, observed flood years and changing drainage conditions.
- Affected: A06 urban planning and transport, Sudan urban-planning strategy, small-farmers recovery, flood/irrigation development backlog.
- **Exact update:** a flood-safety gate for each 5–10-minute services pilot: consult multiple hazard maps, recent community flood history, safe sites/evacuation routes, road/drain maintenance and primary storm-water outfalls; cross-check locally before construction. Low-cost municipal mapping and drain clearing quick win; citywide drainage/land-use regulation five-year; floodplain relocation only lawful, compensated, consent-led and long-term. Skills: drainage engineers, GIS, hydrologists, ward residents; costs of study and measures not provided. Cheaper alternative: field high-water marks, open satellite imagery, vetted HDX layers and local drain inventory before new concrete channels. Risks: over-trusting 97% accuracy, neglecting smaller culverts/flash floods, neglecting informal tenants.

**Mustafa and Hassan (Karary University), “Effective Approach to Assessing and Mitigating Urban Heat Islands for Sustainable Regional Planning: Case Studies from Omdurman, Sudan,” 28 August 2026, DOI 10.5772/intechopen.1016887.**
- Full publisher HTML: https://www.intechopen.com/online-first/1254135 ; **CC BY 4.0** explicitly stated, publisher full HTML and table/methods sections reviewed (not a replication).
- Combines Landsat 8 land surface temperatures, GIS land cover, field comparisons and statistical models across Omdurman. **Important methodological caution:** reported modeled near-surface air temperatures include unusually high values and the article describes some infrared-thermometer methodology ambiguously; these require independent instrument/calibration and study-date verification before quoting absolute temperatures or promising a particular cooling effect. Heat patterns are plausible research hypotheses, not validated block-specific exposure forecasts.
- Affected: urban-planning strategy, A06, solar/cool-roof solution case. **Exact update:** pair any Landsat heat hotspot overlay with *measured* pedestrian air temperature, radiant heat, humidity, shade, wind and indoor roof temperatures; separate hot surface maps from the temperature people actually experience. Skills: university remote-sensing group, municipal enumerators and accessible microclimate probes. Costs: none supplied, low-cost transects possible. Cheaper option: a few shaded/unshaded manual temperature measurement transects and public heat-refuge maps. Quick-win measurement, five-year street retrofits. Risks: remote-sensing/air-temperature confusion, weather-station sparsity, missed disability and water constraints.

## Priority 4: practical implemented municipal-finance case, Sierra Leone

**Local Government Revenue Initiative (LoGRI), “Impact in Sierra Leone: From City Pilot to National Reform Model,” retrospective first-party account, undated page, implementation 2019–2025.**
- https://logri.org/fr/story/impact-in-sierra-leone/ (French path renders English body). Publisher is the implementing/partner organization, **not independent impact evaluator**. No article re-use license found; link only.
- Implemented: initial **11,000-property pilot in two Freetown neighborhoods in 2019**. By later city implementation, property roll **57,000 to ~110,000**; LoGRI reports annual receipts of **~US$500k before reform, $1.5m in 2021, >$2.3m in 2024**, and 39% to 49% compliance 2021–24. Freetown officials were independently administering it by 2025, per implementer. These are reported changes, not peer-reviewed causal effects. No reliable total implementation cost; LoGRI says $4.5m extra revenue over four years exceeded initial donor investment but does not give verifiable capital/operational budget.
- Sudan adaptation: rather than immediately impose taxes in a displaced/war-damaged ward, build *voluntary address and municipal asset registers*, transparent valuations, dispute/appeals mechanisms, protections for low-income households, displacement and informal tenure, electronic receipts, public service-and-spending audits. Separate object mapping from ownership claims. Link any revenue pilot to reliable drains/water/footpaths.
- Institutions/skills: city law and financial authority, land surveyors, GIS, trained ward assessors, treasury auditors, appeals board, public participation, privacy/security.
- Failure risks: coerced taxation, double collection, unsafe/false ownership records, displaced households losing rights, corrupt valuation and politically targeted fines. **Cheaper alternative:** paper/spreadsheet address/property roll, published fee schedules, receipt log and independently checked ward-level budgets before proprietary automation. **Timing:** quick-win one-ward mapping and cash-control; five-year statutory/regulatory and public-finance scale-up; no blanket tax deployment now.
