# Sudan economic reform in the first year of a new government

Research and discussion brief, 9 November 2026. Proposed Kandaka Development topic; not an approved article or adopted government programme.

The working recommendation is to combine emergency stabilization with reforms to public finance, economic power and productive capacity. The first year should make essential services more reliable, protect purchasing power and establish accountable institutions capable of carrying further reforms. A calendar should organize responsibility; readiness tests should govern measures that can raise prices, exclude people or transfer public assets.

Working scenario: a new civilian government with a credible political mandate, uneven territorial access and damaged institutions. A durable peace would widen the agenda. Continued large-scale conflict would require a narrower emergency programme and delivery through accessible local institutions and humanitarian partners. This scenario is an analytical assumption, not a prediction or a user-endorsed constitutional settlement. Four focus questions have been sent to the owner; research can continue while article drafting awaits scope clarification.

## First 90 days

1. **Create a usable emergency budget.** The finance ministry should publish a revenue estimate, an essential-services spending plan and a rolling cash forecast. Record grants, emergency procurement, arrears and central-bank advances. Bring accessible public accounts into a reconciled treasury picture, progressively extending coverage. Publishing a provisional account with stated gaps is preferable to claiming full control over revenues the government cannot reach.

2. **Protect delivery of food, water, health care and basic education.** Prioritize functioning water systems, medicines, essential workers, food distribution and the next agricultural season. Verify payroll records with an appeal route; missing documents should not automatically erase legitimate workers. Provide cash where markets and payment channels function, and food or service support where they do not. Use area-based temporary coverage when precise household targeting would delay assistance or exclude displaced people.

3. **Constrain inflationary financing through a feasible agreement.** The finance ministry and central bank should disclose government borrowing from the bank and agree a declining financing path consistent with actual revenue and committed external support. Any emergency exception needs a ceiling, stated purpose, reporting, expiry and lawful oversight. Monetary discipline requires an accompanying spending and financing plan; an unfunded promise to stop all advances can simply create unpaid wages and suppliers.

4. **Restore payment access and diagnose banks.** Prioritize safe access to deposits and transfers, agent liquidity and payment-system continuity. Begin independent asset-quality and ownership reviews. Separate temporary liquidity shortages from insolvency before committing public recapitalization funds. Avoid a rushed cash replacement or bank closure that cuts off people outside connected cities.

5. **Reduce obstruction to production and trade.** Publish lawful charges and remove unauthorized levies where enforceable, with a plan for replacing legitimate local-service revenue. Prioritize repairs to existing pumps, water facilities, market roads, power supply and communications. Support seeds, veterinary services and repair capacity timed to local seasons. These interventions require itemized procurement and maintenance budgets, not blanket import subsidies.

6. **Start civilian fiscal oversight immediately.** Request registers of state-owned and military-linked commercial assets, public guarantees, gold revenues and major contracts. Require accessible entities to report and transfer legally due revenues. Secure records and prevent unauthorized disposal through lawful procedures. Do not assume a decree can deliver comprehensive control in contested territory.

By day 90, the public should be able to see a provisional budget, actual essential payments, an accessible complaints process, a bank diagnostic timetable and a named institutional owner for each reform. This is a proposed accountability checklist, not a claim that every task is achievable nationwide in three months.

## Months four to six

- **Improve revenue before broadly increasing household burdens.** Review discretionary exemptions and large-taxpayer compliance; reconcile customs and extractive-sector receipts; publish the cost and beneficiaries of exemptions. Do not budget uncertain gold revenues as cash already available. Any tax change needs an incidence assessment, a realistic collection plan and protection for essential consumption and small livelihoods.
- **Make the support system work before changing administered prices.** Test payment receipt, value after inflation, geographic coverage, agent cash availability and grievance resolution. Finance several payment cycles from resources actually committed and usable. Evaluate transport and food-price spillovers. Where transfers cannot compensate reliably, retain a temporary, explicitly budgeted protection mechanism and review it regularly.
- **Prepare exchange-rate reform with financing and bank readiness.** Map actual official and parallel transactions, publish transparent rules and reduce discretionary access to cheap foreign exchange. A move toward a more coherent regime needs a credible budget, remittance channels, reserve and import-financing information, and household protection. Do not promise a fixed rate the authorities cannot finance, or treat an unsupported float as sufficient reform.
- **Establish operating rules for public enterprises.** Publish available accounts, guarantees and quasi-fiscal obligations; subject commercial entities to transparent tax, procurement and competition rules. Assess which deliver essential services and which can be restructured. Ownership changes require verified title, valuation, competition safeguards and lawful oversight.
- **Negotiate external support and debt re-engagement.** Prepare a verified debt and arrears inventory, seek grants and concessional support, and establish with creditors what a renewed debt-relief process requires. Distinguish pledges, signed commitments, disbursements and usable funds. A new cabinet does not automatically restore programme eligibility or complete HIPC debt relief.

The six-month milestone should be demonstrated delivery and better financial information. A subsidy phase-out, currency decision or asset sale should proceed only when its prerequisites are met; it should not be treated as a ceremonial deadline.

## Months seven to twelve

- Adopt a medium-term budget with realistic financing, social-service commitments, regional transfer rules and public debt-risk reporting. Protect execution of priority spending, rather than merely writing a floor into the budget.
- Implement bank resolution or recapitalization case by case after diagnostics, with loss recognition, owner accountability and a credible funding source. Extend practical monetary-policy tools and central-bank accountability. The existing Kandaka monetary article's currency options remain research questions; this brief does not settle gold backing, a currency board or dollarization.
- Begin selected enterprise restructuring under civilian oversight. Require open valuation and procurement, competition review and protection of service continuity and workers. Fiscal transparency can begin early even where ownership restructuring will take longer.
- Scale repairs and agricultural support that show measurable benefit. Evaluate seed availability, animal health, irrigation reliability, storage, transport costs and access to finance by region and gender. Support small firms and producers without assuming that new land concessions or large industrial corridors are the fastest route to recovery.
- Give producers and communities a role in land, water and grazing arrangements. Displacement makes rushed land titling or concessions especially risky. Establish claims and dispute processes before irreversible allocations.
- Publish a year-one review comparing costs, delivery and distributional outcomes with the starting baseline. Explain delays, failures and revisions as well as successes.

## Conditions and measures

| Proposed measure | Readiness test | Reason to slow or revise |
|---|---|---|
| Reduce a consumer subsidy | Verified beneficiaries can obtain adequate support; functioning supply; financed payment cycles; monitored price spillovers | Transfer failures, rising food insecurity, inaccessible regions or loss of essential services |
| Change exchange-rate arrangements | Credible fiscal plan; usable financing; bank exposure assessment; transparent access rules | Unfunded rate defence, severe shortages or unprotected balance-sheet losses |
| Restructure or sell an enterprise | Verified accounts and ownership; independent valuation; competition and worker/service safeguards | Insider bids, disputed assets, monopoly creation or loss of a critical service |
| Recapitalize a bank | Independent diagnosis; loss allocation; governance changes; funded resolution plan | Hidden losses, owner bailout without conditions or unfinanceable guarantees |

Suggested monitoring includes food prices and local supply, real transfer value and successful receipt, essential payroll arrears, central-bank financing, usable reserves, payment failures, reported bank stress, budget execution and regional service access. Establish baselines before assigning numerical targets. Inflation measures, projections and poverty estimates from different institutions are not interchangeable.

## Affordable implementation choices

Begin with existing personnel, reconciled cashbooks, standard procurement forms and public summary accounts; introduce secure digital systems in manageable stages. Test USSD or agent-assisted payments where coverage and cash-out capacity support them, with non-digital alternatives, appeals and data minimization. Repair existing productive equipment before purchasing large replacements. Each option needs lifecycle cost, local technicians, connectivity/energy checks, inclusion outside Khartoum and protection of personal data. These are proposed choices for evaluation, not claims of successful Sudanese deployment. Avoid making a national biometric register or a complex new IT platform a prerequisite for emergency assistance.

## Questions shaping the eventual article

The owner has been asked whether to assume post-war or continuing-conflict government; whether the emphasis should be practical policy or critique of IMF adjustment; which measures are priorities or red lines; and which meta-analysis was remembered. A suitable working focus is **Sudan's first year of economic reform: what must happen before prices and ownership change?** Keep this distinct from the existing monetary-stability article, while cross-referencing its currency research.

The suggested sequence is Kandaka's provisional synthesis. It is not a causal estimate from a meta-analysis, a forecast of implementation capacity or an endorsement of all recommendations in any source. No publication or cover generation is authorized by this research request.
