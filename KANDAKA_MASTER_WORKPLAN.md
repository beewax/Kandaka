# 4. Core Editorial Principles

Kandaka should be:

- empirically grounded
- historically literate
- development-focused
- skeptical of orthodox prescriptions when historical evidence contradicts them
- equally skeptical of naive statism
- strongly focused on implementation
- focused on local ownership and capability-building
- focused on value addition rather than raw commodity export
- focused on accountability
- willing to reject attractive ideas if opportunity cost is too high
- willing to compare Sudan with successful countries without copying them blindly

Standing test for development proposals:

- What does it cost?
- How long does it take?
- What foreign exchange does it require?
- What foreign exchange can it save or earn?
- How many jobs does it create?
- What is the effect on women and youth?
- What is the effect on marginalized regions and communities?
- Can it be decentralized?
- Can Sudan maintain it locally?
- What capabilities does it build?
- What is the opportunity cost?
- What happens if maintenance is imperfect?
- What measurable targets should be published?
- Who is accountable if it fails?

## 17.2 Standing source-use and acquisition requirements

1. **Sudanese evidence first.** Search the relevant ministry, GRAS, ARC, university repository, local journal, professional/community body and historical collection before presenting an imported idea as new. Include regional scholarship beyond Khartoum. Record a search that finds nothing as a search result, not proof that no Sudanese work exists.
2. **Read the actual work.** For every acquired or cited item record exact title, author/institution, publication date and edition, original publication/repository URL, access date, local or catalogue location, rights/reuse terms, duplication status, relevant pages, method/data limits and the RSR IDs used. Distinguish an announcement, strategy, pilot, feasibility study, measured result and independent evaluation. Search for both the original plan and what happened afterward; historical plans, railway records, experiment-station reports and ex-post evaluations are permanent acquisition streams.
3. **Evaluate sources critically.** Government releases, UN reports, development-bank policy recommendations, NGOs, think tanks, companies, academic papers and oral histories all require assessment appropriate to their claims. Examine methods, year, geographic coverage, funding, conflicts, institutional incentives and corroboration. Use bank data and evaluations without automatically adopting their policy prescriptions. Verify NGO financial transparency, actual delivery, local partnerships and coverage; registry inclusion is not donation advice or an endorsement.
4. **Protect rights and provenance.** Lawful access does not automatically permit republication, translation or rehosting. Retain exact edition/page completeness and asset identity. Follow the existing Library source-repair workflow before indiscriminate expansion; do not replace a missing source with a different work or treat a title match as identity verification. Record sensitive community testimony safely and avoid exposing Emergency Response Room identities or operational details.
5. **Keep storage boundaries explicit.** Kandaka's new authorized research uploads belong in the `kandaka-library` Cloudflare R2 bucket. The separate Nile Book Store bucket contains some legacy catalogue URLs but is not the default destination for new Kandaka research. Check each catalogue record, exact key, rights and duplicate status. Do not globally rewrite legacy URLs, change permissions, upload or delete assets as part of this registry synchronization.
6. **Maintain the registry during research.** Add newly discovered credible sources with stable IDs and all fields above; record why they are useful and whether they are primary sources or discovery leads. Update broken endpoints and last-check evidence without silently deleting the institution or claiming that a locator check verifies its findings. ## 17.3 Low-cost and novel-technology research requirement / شرط بحث التقنيات الميسّرة والجديدة

Every relevant development, technology or infrastructure investigation must explicitly look for inexpensive, practical or newly affordable alternatives, including Sudan-developed research that may already exist. Consult ARC, Sudanese universities and applied-research institutions alongside UNESCO, FAO, UNIDO and the technology-strategy streams above. Novelty alone is not a reason to recommend a technology.

Use the **Sudan Technology Opportunity Filter** for every promising candidate: does it solve a defined Sudanese problem; is it useful now or soon and becoming affordable; can Sudan operate, repair and supply it; can it work outside Khartoum; can ordinary people, women, young people, older people and disabled people access it; can it create local skills/jobs, raise productivity, reduce import dependence or save foreign exchange; and is there a cheaper, simpler alternative? Account for institutions, governance, standards, procurement, recurrent costs and maintenance, not only the equipment price.

Record technology maturity (research, prototype, pilot or demonstrated deployment), independent performance evidence, full lifetime cost, imported inputs/foreign-exchange needs, energy/connectivity needs, spare parts, technician training, security/privacy where relevant, environmental/social effects and failure conditions.
