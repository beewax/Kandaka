# 4. Core Editorial Principles

Kandaka should be:

- empirically grounded
- historically literate
- development-focused
- skeptical of orthodox prescriptions when historical evidence contradicts them
- equally skeptical of naive statism
- strongly focused on implementation
- focused on local ownership and capability-building
- focused on value addition rather than raw commodity export
- focused on accountability
- willing to reject attractive ideas if opportunity cost is too high
- willing to compare Sudan with successful countries without copying them blindly

Standing test for development proposals:

- What does it cost?
- How long does it take?
- What foreign exchange does it require?
- What foreign exchange can it save or earn?
- How many jobs does it create?
- What is the effect on women and youth?
- What is the effect on marginalized regions and communities?
- Can it be decentralized?
- Can Sudan maintain it locally?
- What capabilities does it build?
- What is the opportunity cost?
- What happens if maintenance is imperfect?
- What measurable targets should be published?
- Who is accountable if it fails?

## Article 5
### How Should Sudan Rebuild Trust in the Pound?
#### Gold, Dollarization and the Search for Monetary Stability

Status:
DRAFTING / NEEDS CLEAN CONSOLIDATED VERSION

Category:
Development

Cover concept:
Sudan gold / Sudanese monetary imagery.
User selected the first gold-related image previously shown.

Core question:
How can Sudan rebuild trust in its currency after inflation, depreciation, dollarization, and repeated monetary instability?

Options to compare:

- classical gold standard
- gold-backed pound
- reserve-backed pound
- currency board
- dollarization
- euroization
- RMB use
- Gulf currencies
- diversified reserve basket
- currency competition
- inflation targeting
- central bank independence
- temporary hard reserve rule
- redenomination
- Brazil-style transition accounting unit

Country cases:

- Brazil
- Israel
- Bulgaria
- Estonia
- Peru
- Georgia
- Ecuador
- Zimbabwe
- Botswana
- Morocco

Emerging recommendation:
Do not return to a rigid classical gold standard.
Do not permanently dollarize unless national monetary credibility proves impossible.
Investigate a strongly reserve-backed Sudanese pound supported by gold plus diversified reserves, strict limits on routine deficit monetization, independent CBOS, transparent audits, realistic exchange rates, and eventual inflation targeting.

Important user direction:

- main goal: inflation control + public confidence
- gold backing is acceptable without direct citizen convertibility
- compare alternatives fairly
- monetary sovereignty is not automatically valuable if repeatedly abused
- consider geopolitical dependence on any single foreign currency
- use plain language instead of jargon like "geopolitical diversification"
- allow narrow emergency exceptions to anti-deficit-financing rules

## Article 6
### Beyond the Car: How Sudan Should Rebuild Its Cities Around Public Transport
#### BRT, Trambuses, Light Rail, Elevated Rail, Climate-Resilient Streets and a Sudanese Transit Industry

Status:
RESEARCH / THESIS ESTABLISHED / FULL DRAFT NEEDED

Category:
Development

Core thesis:
Sudan should not rebuild cities around private-car dependence. Each city should use the locally maintainable, climate-resilient, universally accessible transport technology that moves the most people per dollar.

Transport hierarchy to evaluate:

- walking
- protected cycling/e-bikes
- electric feeder buses
- electric minibuses
- BRT
- trolleybuses/trambuses
- surface light rail
- elevated light metro
- underground metro only where demand justifies cost
- regional rail

Key design principle:
Public transport should not be trapped in the same traffic as private cars.

Street allocation concept:

`Shaded pedestrian zone | protected bicycle/e-bike lane | dedicated mass-transit corridor | limited general traffic`

Possible dedicated corridor technologies:

- BRT
- trambus/trolleybus
- light rail

Major Sudan-specific design constraints:

- extreme heat
- sandstorms
- dust
- seasonal flooding
- unreliable electricity
- maintenance limitations
- elderly users
- disabled users
- parents with children
- people who cannot walk long distances

Accessibility requirements:

- low-floor boarding
- level boarding
- ramps/lifts
- tactile paving
- audible and visual announcements
- wheelchair space
- frequent shaded seating
- safe crossings
- accessible toilets at major stations
- feeder/paratransit
- short station access distances

User principle:
Design around the person who has the hardest journey.

5–10 minute neighborhood model:

Whenever practical, ordinary residents should be within approximately a 5–10 minute safe, shaded, accessible journey of everyday essentials such as:

- primary school
- clinic/basic healthcare
- groceries/market
- local public services
- places of worship
- parks/community spaces
- public transport

Major hospitals, universities, specialized services and large employment centers should be connected by higher-capacity transit.

Important:
Do not copy European 15-minute-city concepts mechanically.
Use climate-adjusted accessibility.

Local manufacturing / accompanying industries:

Phase 1:
- bus bodies
- seats
- windows
- doors
- handrails
- shelters
- steelwork
- concrete
- cables
- electrical cabinets
- signs
- ticketing hardware

- basic maintenance consumables

Phase 2:

- chargers
- HVAC adapted for Sudan
- vehicle electronics
- wheels/axles
- selected rail components

Phase 3:
- traction equipment
- bogies
- control systems
- local vehicle design if scale justifies it

Accompanying industries:

- steel
- aluminum
- glass
- rubber/tires
- electrical equipment
- batteries
- solar charging
- air conditioning
- software
- payment systems
- signaling
- telecommunications
- civil engineering
- maintenance workshops

Relevant international cases:

- Egypt electric BRT/bus manufacturing
- Turkey / Bozankaya
- Morocco rail localization
- Addis Ababa light rail as warning case
- Dubai elevated metro
- Bangkok elevated rail
- Cairo elevated/metro systems

Sudanese research to cite:

- 2008 Sudan University traffic/transport thesis

- 2025 transit-oriented-development study in Khartoum

Important user direction:

## Article 7
### Should Sudan Restore Its Independence Flag?

Status:
IDEA / THESIS ESTABLISHED

Category:
Ideas

Core thesis:
The blue-yellow-green 1956 independence flag deserves serious consideration as a post-war national symbol because it is historically Sudanese, visually distinctive, and predates the current Pan-Arab-style flag.

Preferred direction:
Restore 1956 design essentially unchanged.

Reasons:

- original independence flag
- visually distinctive
- less easily confused with other Arab flags
- not associated with a later military regime
- inexpensive symbolic reset
- historically grounded
- avoids creating an entirely new flag

Counterarguments:

- current flag has represented Sudan for more than half a century
- generations identify with it
- flag change is symbolic, not developmental
- should not substitute for institutional reform

Important user position:
User strongly favors restoring the independence flag.
