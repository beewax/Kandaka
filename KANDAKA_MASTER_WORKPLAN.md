# 4. Core Editorial Principles

Kandaka should be:

- empirically grounded
- historically literate
- development-focused
- skeptical of orthodox prescriptions when historical evidence contradicts them
- equally skeptical of naive statism
- strongly focused on implementation
- focused on local ownership and capability-building
- focused on value addition rather than raw commodity export
- focused on accountability
- willing to reject attractive ideas if opportunity cost is too high
- willing to compare Sudan with successful countries without copying them blindly

Standing test for development proposals:

- What does it cost?
- How long does it take?
- What foreign exchange does it require?
- What foreign exchange can it save or earn?
- How many jobs does it create?
- What is the effect on women and youth?
- What is the effect on marginalized regions and communities?
- Can it be decentralized?
- Can Sudan maintain it locally?
- What capabilities does it build?
- What is the opportunity cost?
- What happens if maintenance is imperfect?
- What measurable targets should be published?
- Who is accountable if it fails?
