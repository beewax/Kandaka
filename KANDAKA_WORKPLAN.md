## How to maintain this file

Use these standard fields for every article, Library summary, acquisition, and site change:

- **Research:** Not started / In progress / Complete / Refresh required
- **Draft:** Not started / Outline / Partial / Complete
- **Review:** Not ready / Awaiting review / Changes requested / Complete
- **Approved:** No / Yes
- **Published:** No / Scheduled YYYY-MM-DD / Yes YYYY-MM-DD
- **Next action:** One concrete next step

When adding an item, preserve the approved thesis and requirements; append new decisions under `Notes/requirements` rather than silently replacing earlier ones. Dates are proposals until the article is individually approved.

## Efficient collaboration and token-use policy

The goal is to reduce repetition and waiting time without reducing research, verification, editorial judgment, bilingual quality, or publication safeguards.

1. Treat this workplan as the canonical project memory. Begin from the relevant item ID (for example, `A01` or `L02`) and read only the sections and source files needed for that task.
2. The user can give compact instructions such as: `Continue A01: update the evidence, return only changed claims and the clean review draft.` There is no need to paste earlier conversations when the decision is already recorded here.
3. Default to concise progress updates: report only material findings, changed assumptions, blockers, decisions needed, test results, and the next action. Do not repeat the full project history in every response.
4. Research in two passes: first retrieve the strongest primary and authoritative sources; then run targeted checks only for gaps, contradictions, and time-sensitive claims. Avoid broad repeated searches that do not change the conclusion.
5. Reuse the existing source ledger, citations, image records, article requirements, and prior verified findings. Recheck facts when they are current, disputed, high-stakes, or central to the argument—not merely because a new chat began.
6. Keep routine repository work together: make related edits in one branch and pull request, run proportionate checks once, and report the combined result. Separate unrelated or high-risk changes.
7. Use a concise answer by default. Request a full research memo, source-by-source audit, or step-by-step explanation only when it will affect an editorial decision or provide a reusable deliverable.
8. Start a new task when the subject changes substantially, but identify the workplan item and desired output. This keeps each task focused while preserving continuity through this file.
9. Approval shortcuts never apply to publication. Saving tokens must not remove source verification, counterarguments, Arabic review, image-rights checks, accessibility checks, or explicit article-specific approval.

## Editorial rules already established

1. Kandaka should be empirically grounded, historically literate, readable by an intelligent general audience, and willing to challenge fashionable orthodoxy.
2. Do not automatically agree with an article idea. Test its priority, feasibility, cost, opportunity cost, institutional demands, and alternatives. Reject or lower the priority of weak proposals.
3. Favor value addition over raw commodity exports; local ownership and capability-building; strategic but accountable state action; deliberate sequencing; decentralization; maintenance of existing assets; and measurable performance.
4. International contractors and imports should transfer skills, documentation, maintenance capacity, and a pathway toward local production.
5. Do not romanticize either the state or markets. Assess what worked, what failed, why it failed, and who benefited.
6. Treat peace, inclusion, regional equality, women and youth employment, accessibility for elderly and disabled people, and environmental/community safeguards as development requirements rather than footnotes.
7. Distinguish claims from evidence. In particular, distinguish mineral occurrences, resources, reserves, recoverability, and commercial feasibility.
8. Prefer original data, plans, surveys, evaluations, feasibility studies, and primary documents. Press releases may inform research but ordinarily do not belong in the Library.
9. Before selecting a Library work for a full summary, inventory existing Kandaka summaries and verify that the work has not already received the full analytical treatment.
10. Every substantive factual claim, number, comparison, and current organization recommendation must be checked against the underlying source and refreshed close to publication.

## Proposed publication cadence and workflow

**Proposed cadence:** One substantive article every three days, alternating subjects so Kandaka does not become economics-only. The earlier provisional sequence was September 1, 4, 7, and 10, 2026; those dates have passed and must be rescheduled rather than reused automatically.

Required workflow for each article:

1. Confirm the user's thesis and non-negotiable requirements.
2. Research and fact-check the English article.
3. Produce a complete English review draft marked `DRAFT — NOT APPROVED FOR PUBLICATION`.
4. Obtain explicit, article-specific approval.
5. Make final corrections.
6. Produce the Arabic edition.
7. Prepare English and Arabic site files, metadata, licensed images, and social preview.
8. Schedule only after approval; verify the live publication and promotion.

Approval of one article never approves another. If an article is not ready or approved, move its date; do not publish an unfinished piece merely to preserve cadence.

### Acquisition criteria

Prioritize documents containing original data, strategies, feasibility studies, surveys, evaluations, audits, historically important plans, and evidence of implementation or failure. Search for paired records: an early feasibility study plus a later evaluation may be more valuable than a current overview. Do not download everything a ministry publishes. Press releases generally remain research leads, not Library entries.

For every acquisition record: exact title; author/institution; date/version; original URL; local/R2 location; file format; language; page count; rights/access note; checksum if practical; Library duplication check; short relevance note; and status.

## Image and visual requirements

1. Use authentic Sudan-related images wherever possible; avoid generic stock imagery that could represent any country.
2. Verify source, creator, license, attribution text, and whether edits/crops are allowed before publication.
3. Exclude South Sudan material unless the article explicitly concerns South Sudan. Do not rely on keyword matching alone; visually and contextually verify the subject.
4. Every article should have a horizontal cover suitable for the site and social preview. Create a separate vertical Pinterest treatment when useful rather than placing headline text over the article cover.
5. Captions must say what is actually shown and avoid using illustrative images as proof of historical continuity, mineral reserves, institutional performance, or current conditions.
6. Prefer images showing Sudanese agency, production, culture, institutions, landscapes, and historical evidence over repetitive war/poverty imagery unless conflict is the subject.
7. Store stable image metadata alongside the article: source URL, creator, license, attribution, alt text, caption, crop notes, and download date.
8. Specific pending needs are recorded under A01, A03, A04, and A05. The transport article will need a clear comparison graphic or map only if it materially improves the argument.

## Research Watch and update policy

### Research Watch

- Maintain a permanent watch on Sudanese ministry, CBOS, CBS, GRAS, university, public-corporation, international-institution, and historical-archive sources.
- Favor new primary documents, newly digitized historical material, revised datasets, feasibility/evaluation pairs, and material that corrects or tests an existing Kandaka claim.
- Record a lead even when it is not acquired immediately, but do not call it a Library holding until the actual file is secured and cataloged.
- For current affairs, humanitarian organizations, prices, laws, officeholders, programs, and operating access, refresh research immediately before publication.
- When a new source changes a thesis, update the article openly; do not force the evidence to fit the earlier idea.

### Progress updates

- Updates should state: what changed, what was verified, important findings/numbers, blockers, decisions needed, and the next concrete action.
- Never imply background work is continuing when no automation or active task is actually running.
- The earlier concept was a concise daily research update and an editorial advancement every three days. Treat these as policy proposals; verify any current automation separately before relying on it.
- When the user says `go ahead`, execute the available work and return a substantive result rather than only describing the next step.

### Publication safety

- No automatic publication from the research watch.
- No draft is approved merely because its thesis is established or the research is complete.
- No translation or scheduling substitutes for editorial approval.
- Verify the live page after publication and confirm bilingual links, images, categories, metadata, and share previews.
